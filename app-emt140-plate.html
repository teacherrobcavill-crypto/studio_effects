1957 EMT-140 Engineering Guide How 2D Flexural Plate Dispersion, Damper Gap & Dual Piezo Pickups Work ▼ Unfold Guide
A moving-coil driver mounted slightly off-center excites 2D bending waves in the tensioned metal sheet. Because flexural phase velocity increases with frequency ($v_p \propto \sqrt{\omega}$), high-frequency transients reach the two asymmetric piezo contact pickups before low frequencies—creating the unmistakable dense "whip" that made the EMT-140 famous on Abbey Road, Pink Floyd, and Motown vocals.

History, Physics & Math 1930s Bell Labs → 1939 Hammond Oil Tubes → 1960 Accutronics Type 4 → 1961 6G15 vs. 1963 AB763 & Wittrick Wave Equations ▼ Unfold Equations & Topologies
1. Historical & Developmental Timeline:
  • 1930s (Bell Labs): Mechanical delay lines researched to simulate long-distance telephone network delay.
  • 1939 (Laurens Hammond): Massive springs suspended in oil-filled tubes to dampen mechanical response and shorten decay for the Hammond organ.
  • 1959/1960 (Accutronics / Alan Young): Compact dry Type 4 dual-spring tank with incommensurate delay times to minimize standing waves and flutter echoes.
  • 1961 (Fender 6G15 Standalone) vs. 1963 (AB763 Built-in): High-headroom 6K6 pentode pre-amp unit vs. built-in 12AT7 combo amp circuit with speaker-to-spring acoustic feedback.
